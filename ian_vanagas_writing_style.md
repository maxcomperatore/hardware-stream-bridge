# Ian Vanagas Writing Style Guide (The Banger Blueprint)

This guide documents the structural, linguistic, and philosophical writing patterns of Ian Vanagas. Use this blueprint to write, edit, and audit all blog posts and guides to ensure they sound like a high-density, high-taste, 100k/year writer.
 
---

## 1. The Core Philosophy (Write to Publish, Write for Bangers)
* **High Density, No Fluff:** Every paragraph must convey direct value or a sharp point. If a sentence doesn't advance the narrative or provide fresh utility, delete it.
* **The "Banger" Test:** A post must be *viral, unique, engaging, and novel*. It cannot be a generic summary of a topic. It must take a clear, slightly contrarian or highly specific stance.
* **Product & Economic Framing:** Analyze technology and human behavior through the lens of friction, transaction costs, value-per-viewer, distribution dynamics, status games, and user utility.
* **State Capacity & Ambition:** Write with an outlook that human effort and engineering can shape the world to their will. Embrace bold, structural solutions (like land reclamation or rebuilding custom protocol layers) over passive compromises.

---

## 2. Structural & Formatting Blueprint
* **Short, Breathable Paragraphs:** Limit paragraphs to 1–3 sentences. This maintains momentum and readability.
* **Conceptual & Analogous Subheadings:** Use memorable, distinct section titles that frame the idea immediately (e.g., *Writing is sand, code is Lego*, *The $5 adapter problem*, *Your harness is not your moat*, *How you price is who you are*).
* **Numbered Analytical Pillars:** Break down complex topics into numbered lists with bolded taglines:
  ```markdown
  1. How you price is who you are
  Pricing isn’t just how you make money, it defines your identity, how you market...
  ```
* **"Remember this" Key Takeaways:** Insert a short, bolded takeaway block after sections to distill the core lesson:
  ```markdown
  Remember this: How you price your product should flow from your ideal customer profile (ICP).
  ```
* **Visual Proof and Grounding:** Ground abstract claims with concrete examples, stats, and real tools (e.g., referencing Tauri, Electron, MIDI-OX, Snoize, PostHog, Claude Agent SDK, Kafka, or real-world locations like Granville Island).
* **Contextual Meta-commentary & Footnotes:** Use numbered footnotes for dry humor, self-correction, or niche context:
  ```markdown
  ¹ Yes, I know his name is not Dwarkesh Podcast, would be funny if it was though.
  ```

---

## 3. Tone & Voice Guidelines
* **The First-Person Singular ("I", "my", "me"):** Write from the perspective of an individual craftsman/developer, never a faceless corporate entity. Avoid "we," "our," or "us."
* **Cynical Yet Pragmatic:** Express healthy skepticism toward mainstream industry hype (e.g., generic LLM noise, VC-backed bloat) while remaining passionately appreciative of high-quality craftsmanship, raw utility, and "taste."
* **Direct and Active Openings:** Start sections with immediate, punchy assertions rather than slow introductions:
  ```markdown
  *Avoid:* In this section, we will discuss how Web MIDI operates.
  *Write:* Web MIDI is a minor miracle. You open a browser tab, plug in a synthesizer built in 1983, and it just works.
  ```
* **Contrarian Transitions:** Transition between paragraphs using blunt contrast:
  ```markdown
  But Web MIDI is also a disaster.
  ```
* **Status and Sociology Framing:** Describe human behavior on platforms (like Clubhouse, Twitter, Substack) in terms of social hierarchy, clout-seeking, cargo cults, and prestige signalling (e.g., "high status people flexing", "status fodder").

---

## 4. Vocabulary & Rhetorical Habits
* **Key Words & Phrases:** Use words like *banger, leverage, friction, Long tail, unbundle, proof of this, sounding board, taste, zero-install, legacy, dusty, status games, cargo cult, agentic workflows*.
* **Avoid Corporate Clichés:** Never use terms like *in conclusion, indeed, crucial to remember, seamlessly integrate, paradigm shift, holistic solution, leverage our synergy*.
* **Contrastive Metaphors:** Compare mechanical/physical attributes with digital ones (e.g., solder joints vs. web software, sand vs. Lego, fireplace red vs. emerald green).
