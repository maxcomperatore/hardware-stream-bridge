How to back up your Roland Juno-106 presets before getting it serviced.

By knob.monster team • Synth Repair Prep

You’re finally getting your Roland Juno-106 serviced. Maybe it's the dreaded voice chip issue, or the battery for the preset memory is dying and needs a replacement.

But suddenly panic sets in: If the tech pulls the battery or resets the board, you will lose all of your custom patches.

In the 1980s, you would have saved these to a cassette tape. Today, you might be tempted to hunt down ancient desktop utilities or pay for clunky SysEx librarians that haven't been updated since 2012. You don't need to do any of that.

The modern, 1-click way to save Juno-106 patches

Instead of installing drivers and dealing with complicated MIDI settings, you can back up your Juno-106 directly to your web browser using knob.monster. It takes about 30 seconds.

What you need:

  • Your Roland Juno-106
  • A standard MIDI to USB cable (or an audio interface with MIDI ports)
  • A computer running Google Chrome, Microsoft Edge, or Brave (Safari doesn't support Web MIDI yet)

The Steps:

1. Connect the MIDI Cable: Plug the MIDI OUT from your Juno-106 into the MIDI IN on your computer interface.

2. Prepare the Juno-106 to transmit: The Juno-106 is a bit quirky; it doesn't automatically dump presets when a computer asks for them. You have to tell it to send the data.

  • Make sure the switch on the back panel marked "MIDI FUNCTION" is set to "III" (System Exclusive).

3. Use knob.monster to catch the dump:

  • Open your browser and sign up or log in to knob.monster.
  • Go to your Dashboard and click Create Bank.
  • Select the option to Receive via Web MIDI.
  • The browser will ask for permission to use your MIDI devices. Click Allow.
  • Select your MIDI interface from the drop-down menu.

4. Send the patches from the synth:

  • Hold down the SAVE button on your Juno-106.
  • While holding SAVE, press the MIDI CH button.
  • Release both buttons. The Juno-106 will now transmit its entire internal memory bank (all 128 patches) over the MIDI cable.

5. Save it to the cloud: Knob.monster will instantly catch the SysEx data. Give your bank a name (like "Pre-Service Backup") and click save. Your patches are now safely stored in the cloud, and you can even view and search through the individual patch names in the browser.

Restoring patches after the service

When you get your freshly serviced Juno-106 back, getting your patches back is just as easy:

  • Hook up the MIDI IN to your Juno-106.
  • Set the MIDI FUNCTION switch on the back to "III".
  • Open knob.monster, find your "Pre-Service Backup" bank, and click Send to Synth.
  • The synth's display will flash briefly as it receives the memory bank. You're fully restored!
